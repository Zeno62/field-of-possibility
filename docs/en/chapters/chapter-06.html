6 | Life Is Not a Level of Intelligence

A caterpillar does not first have to say “I” to be called alive.

Now imagine a machine that writes poetry, proves theorems, and talks with you at length about loneliness and death. The more familiar its words, the more readily we sense a “someone” there, undergoing all of this.

One cannot describe itself in language; the other can speak of itself repeatedly. Rank life by that scale, and the caterpillar would have to prove its credentials to the machine.

The problem lies in the scale.

Intelligence is not a license for life.

Language, reasoning, planning, and self-description tell us what a structure can do. The strength of those abilities does not directly explain how a life-state becomes established.

The Same Essence, Different Forms

The previous chapter returned consciousness to the known. We now need to distinguish the relation between consciousness and life.

Field of Possibility distinguishes the essence of life from an ordinary life-state:

Essence of life = Luminosity = Awareness = Pure Possibility = the sole Knowing

Ordinary life-state = the manifested state of life when a genuine
high-constraint consciousness subnet is established

The first line concerns essence, the second manifested structure.

This does not assemble Luminosity and consciousness into life. The establishment of a consciousness subnet is this relational organization manifesting as appearance and being known. It does not first wait in darkness for Luminosity to illuminate it. Nor does Luminosity divide into privately owned shares according to different bodies and experiences.

Stones and people both belong to manifestation; neither has ever been apart from Knowing. Their difference does not concern which has received illumination, but what kind of relational structure is established.

In this book, the specific condition for an ordinary life-state is the establishment of a genuine high-constraint consciousness subnet. “High-constraint” means that particular relations must sustain it together. It is not an intelligence score, nor does the name alone explain every mechanism of consciousness.

A living being can therefore have very simple intelligence, while a simulated consciousness-image can display powerful cognitive abilities. This does not put them on the same ladder toward life.

For images formed by consciousness, the book adopts the following judgment about their relations:

Consciousness can create images, but it cannot create life.

This framework classifies ordinary AI as a simulated consciousness-image formed by living consciousness, not a lower stage of life. The judgment is not based on its being human-made or on its use of silicon. Chapter 9 separates the premise, the conclusions drawn from it, and the relational conditions that still require explanation.

The Essence of Life Does Not Mean the Person Continues Forever

Memory changes, the body changes, and so do the relations sustaining a particular consciousness. Changes in these relations change the state of consciousness. When the organization sustaining it ceases to hold altogether, that particular consciousness disappears.

“The essence of life neither arises nor ceases” refers to Luminosity. Whether a person’s memories, personality, and particular consciousness continue is a different question. A claim about essence does not guarantee personal permanence.

Appearances differ in countless ways; all are alike in being known. Life-states may vary; the sole Knowing never divides.

Making these distinctions, however, does not mean that the everyday “I” has lost its power.

If a judgment about “me” is only something known, why can it keep governing feeling and action?
