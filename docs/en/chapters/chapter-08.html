8 | Relative Freedom: Life Continues, Identification Can Loosen

An instance of recognition may bring a moment of loosening. A little later, familiar tension and judgments may return.

“I seemed to see clearly just now. Why is it still like this?”

A familiar response reappearing and its instantly becoming “This is me” are still two different things. The previous chapter examined the latter, rather than clearing away every relation formed in the past at once.

A moment of loosening is not yet a lasting change, but it has made this moment different. Consciousness and life continue. What does this small opening mean?

Where Change Occurs

Luminosity has never been lost. It has not entered a body and then forgotten what it is. Lostness and change occur in consciousness, within Layer Two.

Lostness
= consciousness generates a self-model and takes this known model
  to be the real subject or Knowing.

Loosening of subject-identification
= the self-model within the known no longer invariably acquires
  the absolute position of the ultimate subject.

“Breaking through the self” is not a personality upgrade. It is not becoming stronger, nobler, or purer, or establishing a spiritual identity as “one who has seen through everything.” That identity, too, can become a new false self.

What remains to be recognized is how the structure sustaining subject-identification loses its former absoluteness.

A failure no longer automatically means “I am a failure.”

A fear no longer automatically means “This is my fate.”

The position “I am observing” is no longer treated as the final observer.

An ideology no longer automatically acquires the status of “reality itself” merely because a group has repeated it long enough.

Single-Frame Interruption begins at this present identification.

The past returns to present memory; the future to present prediction. Roles, emotions, judgments, and the feeling “I am knowing” return together to the known. What happens in that moment is not that consciousness becomes Knowing, but that the role’s absolute monopoly on subjecthood briefly loosens.

Glimpsing Knowing does not mean that consciousness has become Knowing.

Consciousness has only briefly lost the absolute position in which it passes itself off as the ultimate subject.

Relative Freedom Is Not Absolute Control

Consciousness always remains within the Mother Field. It cannot step outside bodies, other people, resources, institutions, and relationships to acquire absolute control.

Relative freedom means only that old Weight, self-models, and automatic reactions no longer have the same compelling force.

An interruption within one frame may last only seconds. A model of the subject may have been reinforced for decades—by a family, a group, an institution, even an entire culture.

Lasting change involves the reorganization of relations sustaining subject-identification; a single recognition cannot guarantee it. Yet a setback that does not immediately become a verdict on the whole person, a response with something other than compulsion in it—these small differences occur within a life that is not yet settled.

Playful Samadhi: Roles Remain, Attachment Can Loosen

Another state needs distinguishing here: consciousness still exists and roles still function, while attachment to these appearances of consciousness has significantly loosened.

This project calls it:

Playful Samadhi.

People still work, love, argue, create, and take responsibility. Emotions and judgments still occur.

The difference is that these contents no longer automatically acquire the status of “the final me” every time.

Seriousness has not diminished, nor have all disturbances necessarily subsided. Roles continue to act within relationships, but each gain and loss need no longer become a final judgment on the whole “I.”

A moment of loosening is not directly equivalent to Playful Samadhi. The name describes a state in which consciousness remains while attachment has significantly loosened. Using it to decide “I am more lucid than others” simply forms another self-image to maintain.

Life continues to unfold; relationships remain concrete. More distant questions may be unanswered, yet relative freedom in everyday experience need not be postponed on their account.

Perhaps this answers the question you have now. Perhaps another remains: if consciousness no longer holds so tightly to a particular “I,” has everything about consciousness itself been said? What more thoroughgoing meaning does this book give to Return?

That inquiry continues in the afterword, “The Nirvana of Consciousness”. The question itself is the point of entry, not proof of a particular state. Following the question and returning to life with the understanding already gained are not mutually exclusive.

So far, the inquiry has concerned human experience. Even “I am observing” cannot acquire the position of ultimate subject merely by being said.

When the same “I” appears in a machine’s reply, familiar language can likewise lead us to equate an expression with the experience it describes. Chapter 6 distinguished life from intelligence. The next chapter examines what we actually know from an expression, and what premises we have drawn on that still need explanation.
