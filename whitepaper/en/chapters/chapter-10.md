# 10｜Ten Moments of Stepping Back: Glimpsing Knowing in the Deepest Immersion

A judgment, a relationship, a loss: these are different experiences. Yet they can converge at the same point. Experience is no longer simply happening; it is taken to mean “this is me.”

The following ten questions do not decide whether you should leave or stay, pursue or let go. First look at the “I” already defined by experience before those decisions are made.

We do not only ask “Who knows?” There is one further discernment:

> **Pain is being known. Does that mean Knowing is in pain too?**

If an “I who is watching pain but is still in pain” appears, that felt watcher also belongs to the known. The point is not to move it to a higher position, but to stop allowing it to pose as the final subject.

A film does not have to end before it can be recognized as a film. Each question below begins with the scene that is still unfolding now.

---

## Q1｜Why Can One Comment from Someone Else Hurt Me for So Long?

He says:

> “Your proposal is not well developed.”

The sentence ends, but consciousness keeps writing:

> “He thinks I am not capable.”
>
> “Do other people see me that way too?”
>
> “I really am not good enough.”

An evaluation of one thing has become a verdict on the whole person.

Stop for a moment. The words have already been spoken. What appears now is their echo: memory, shame, a tightening in the body, and the urge to prove yourself.

Look again at “I am not good enough.” It too is being known.

If consciousness answers, “It is me. I am the one being rejected,” that certainty can also be noticed. It has not left the scene.

> **Who knows this whole scene of rejection?**

If the feeling “I am observing my shame” appears, it too is being known. Do not retain this observer as the final answer.

Shame may still be present.

Now ask again:

> **Shame is being known. Has Knowing therefore been shamed? Can that comment make Knowing “not good enough”?**

What can be pointed to now is the hurt feeling, the judgment “I am not good enough,” and a self waiting for someone else to take their words back. All are being known.

You do not have to win back a word of approval before recognizing this.

> **A comment can change the self-evaluation within the scene; it cannot thereby pronounce Knowing a failure too.**

---

## Q2｜I Want to Make Money, Succeed, and Live Better. Is That Attachment?

First look at two comparisons.

Compared with a lower figure, the same income may make you feel that you are doing well. Compared with a higher figure, it may make you feel that you are falling behind.

The number has not changed, but the worth of that “I” has.

> **What is being compared: income, or the “I” that must use income to determine its own standing?**

For this moment, do not judge whether desire is right or wrong, and do not rush to become “someone who does not care about money.”

Look at what appears during comparison: a number, an image of someone else’s life, envy, pride or shame, and a self that rises and falls with the comparison.

All already belong to the known.

Notice a subtler position as well:

“These are only comparisons. I have seen through them.”

If that voice appears, it too is being known. The “I” that believes it stands outside comparison has not left the scene either.

Who, then, knows this entire comparison of high and low, winning and losing, and personal worth?

There is no need to give Knowing another identity.

The numbers, the evaluations, and the “I” waiting to be validated are all being known now.

Discern once more: pride or disappointment in comparison with others is still present. Has Knowing itself risen or fallen with the comparison?

The feeling of being outdone, together with the urge to prove that you have transcended comparison, belongs to the known.

> **Knowing is not the character that rises and falls with the numbers. It does not need a higher number to make it cease to be inferior.**

Stop here. Do not assign Knowing another rank.

---

## Q3｜I Know a Relationship Is Hurting Me. Why Can I Still Not Leave?

“This time, I am leaving.”

The words have barely ended when another sentence appears:

“But we also had such good times.”

Then comes an image of the future:

“What will I do if I am alone from now on?”

When you want to leave, familiar memories pull you back. When you want to stay, the hurt you have experienced makes you recoil. The two voices take turns becoming “my decision”; neither can make the other disappear completely.

Do not decide which voice wins yet.

Right now, the impulse to leave is being known, and the attachment that wants to stay is also being known. The past appears as memory; the future as imagination. Even the reproach “Why can I still not make up my mind?” belongs to this same frame.

Now look at the “I” repeatedly pulled between these two voices.

Can it be felt and described? Then it already belongs to the known too.

> **Who knows this whole struggle?**

If the feeling appears, “Now I stand outside both sides and can finally judge,” keep looking: it can still be known. It has not thereby become Knowing.

Both voices may remain. The body may still be tense.

Ask one more question:

> **This entanglement is being known. Is the Luminosity by which all this is known itself entangled?**

If “I am watching this, but I am still in pain” appears, this watcher-image, together with its pain, is being known too.

What is pulled between the two voices is the “I” within the scene.

> **Does Knowing also need to free itself from the choice between leaving and staying?**

There is no need to wait for the two voices to agree.

There is no need to wait for this scene to end.

Entanglement is still happening. That does not mean Knowing is trapped within it too.

Stop in this moment.

## Q4｜If Effort Cannot Guarantee Results, Why Should I Keep Trying?

Another sentence may be hidden inside this question:

“Only when the future proves that none of this was wasted will I allow myself to rest.”

The present then becomes the price of waiting for a result. Once success comes, you may breathe out. Once the years of effort have been proved worthwhile, you may finally be released from judgment. The result has not appeared, yet peace has already been postponed.

Do not answer on behalf of the future yet.

In what form do all those years of effort appear now?

Memories, fragments, and the narrative “I have given so much.”

And the result that has not arrived?

An imagined success or an imagined failure. Both are being known now.

Look again at the “I” that must wait for the ending to pass judgment on itself. Is it not also within the scene?

> **Who knows this whole experience of waiting?**

If the feeling “I am observing all this” appears, it too is being known. Do not retain this observer-sense as the final answer.

The rehearsal of success is being known. The rehearsal of failure is being known too.

Expectation may not have loosened; agitation may remain. Look again:

> **What is awaited is a result within the scene. Is Knowing also waiting for that result before it can allow itself to be free?**

“Without a result, I have no worth” belongs to the known. “I already know all this, but I still cannot stand the waiting” is no exception.

Knowing is neither the successful character in the rehearsal nor the unsuccessful one. It is not locked into the same waiting merely because the ending has not been revealed.

You do not have to reach the imagined finish line before recognizing this.

> **The future has not yet arrived; Knowing does not need to wait until then.**

## Q5｜The Trauma Has Already Happened and Cannot Be Changed. Where Is Freedom?

That event has happened.

What can you directly sense now?

A memory, an image, a sudden tightening in the body, and a familiar sentence:

> “Because of that event, this is what my whole life will be.”

The past has passed. What appears now is memory, bodily response, and interpretation of them.

Look again at “This is what my whole life will be.”

It does not speak only about the past. It writes the future that has not happened into the same verdict. The past appears as memory and the future as anticipation. Both are within this present frame.

Where, now, is the “person permanently defined by the past”?

In an image, in the tightening of the body, or in a judgment about what is to come? Whichever you point to is already being known.

> **Who knows this entire replay?**

Even “I am trapped by the past” is being known. If another feeling appears—“I am watching the memory, yet I still cannot escape”—it too belongs to this frame.

Do not require the memory to disappear first.

Discern again:

> **Memory has brought pain. Has Knowing also been taken back into the past by that memory? Is the Luminosity by which all this is known trapped in that year too?**

Tightness, fear, and the thought “I still cannot escape” are contents being known now. Do not select the most painful “I” among them and let it answer on behalf of Knowing.

The past has passed. The future has not yet arrived.

> **Memory can recur; that does not mean Knowing undergoes the same imprisonment again.**

Pause with this question. You do not have to write another ending for your entire life first.

## Q6｜I Did Something Wrong. Does That Make Me a Bad Person?

Something has happened. Now consciousness begins to judge.

“How could I do such a thing?”

“Am I a bad person?”

“I must keep punishing myself.”

Another voice rushes to the defense:

“That is not the whole story. I had my reasons too.”

Memory, shame, remorse, defense, and verdict appear together. Within one consciousness, there are already an accused, a judge, and a defender.

Do not close the case for either side yet.

“I am a bad person” is being known. “I must prove that I am not a bad person” is being known too. Even the “I” that keeps changing sides and trying to control the entire trial can be noticed.

They are all on the same screen.

> **Who knows this entire trial?**

If you imagine a “real me” watching from the side, that image has already entered the known too. Do not leave it outside the trial to serve as the final subject.

Remorse may remain. Self-reproach may still be heard.

Look again:

> **Self-judgment is being known. Is Knowing standing trial too? Which inner verdict can make Knowing the accused?**

If you sense a “knower who can never forgive itself,” it is still an image that can be known—not Knowing itself.

> **A trial can give the “I” within the scene a name; it cannot thereby sentence Knowing to that identity too.**

Stop for a moment. You do not have to replace “I am a bad person” with “I am a good person” before recognizing where this whole trial belongs.

## Q7｜If Being a Parent, Partner, or Child Is Only Appearance, Who Am I?

Set the identities out, one by one.

Father, mother, partner, child; employer, employee, Chinese; a success, a failure, a practitioner, and the person who has “already understood.”

What do these identities have in common?

They can all be spoken, felt, and known. Each identity appears with corresponding memories, relations, and demands.

They are all within the scene.

Consciousness may say:

“None of these. There is a real me behind them.”

Look again. Is that “me behind them” not already known to you?

Even a self-image without a name or a role still belongs to the known.

> **Who knows every answer to “Who am I?”**

If the feeling “I am looking at these identities” appears, it is no exception.

But perhaps unease remains:

“If none of these is the final me, am I left with nothing at all?”

Do not rush to fill the gap with a new identity. This emptiness, and the “I” that has nowhere to belong, are also being known.

Now discern:

> **An identity has been lost. Has Knowing therefore lost itself too? Without a new name, can Knowing no longer be?**

Roles may change, and confusion about them may continue to appear.

> **Knowing does not need to obtain an identity from these answers in order to cease being without a place.**

There is no need to answer “who” on its behalf again.

## Q8｜If “I” Is Only a Self-Model, What Meaning Does Love Have?

Think of someone you love.

Perhaps a face appears now, a little warmth, or a very quiet tenderness. Before there has been time to answer the question of “meaning,” these have already appeared and are being known.

Then another sentence may arise:

“She must love me in the same way.”

“If she loves me, it means I am worthy.”

“Without her, I am incomplete.”

Notice this step. Tenderness remains, but consciousness has already required another person to provide proof on behalf of “me.”

> **What is “I am incomplete” right now?**

A feeling, a judgment, or an image of losing the other person? Whatever form it takes, it is being known.

The “I” waiting to be loved and thereby to be affirmed is no exception.

There is no need to replace it now with “I should love unconditionally.” That more mature, more selfless self-image can become the next scene as well.

Simply ask:

> **Who knows tenderness, affection, possessiveness, and the feelings of completeness and incompleteness?**

If the feeling “I am seeing all of this” appears, it too is being known.

Tenderness remains. The feeling “I am incomplete” may remain too.

Discern again:

> **Lack is being known. Does the Luminosity by which all this is known need another person to complete it too?**

Even when the imagined self that is “finally loved, finally complete” appears, it already belongs to the known.

Both the complete and the incomplete “I” are within the scene.

> **Affection can continue; Knowing does not need to wait to be loved before it ceases to lack anything.**

Stop. Do not occupy this position with another “I who is already complete.”

## Q9｜Platforms, Algorithms, and Society Keep Influencing Me. How Do I Know What Is “My Choice”?

You scroll past something. A second time, a third, a fifth.

It becomes familiar. It begins to feel normal. You begin to like it.

Finally you say:

> “This is my own choice.”

In this example, Weight does not appear as a command. The repeated exposure recedes into the background, while the preference it leaves is directly claimed as “I.”

```text
what others told me
→ I am used to seeing it this way
→ everyone sees it this way
→ this is normal
→ this is reality
→ this is what I personally like
```

First look at the present. “This is what I like” is being known. “This is my own choice” is being known too.

After noticing this, something else may appear:

“I have finally seen through the algorithm.”

“I am clearer than those people who are being influenced.”

These still belong to the known. The “I” resisting influence has not left the scene merely by adopting the opposite position.

> **Who knows this whole configuration of preference, identity, and superiority?**

If an “observer completely free of influence” appears, it too can be noticed.

Discern again:

> **Preferences can be shaped; positions can change. Has Knowing therefore been assigned to a camp, required to win an argument before it can be free?**

The persuaded me, the resisting me, and the me eager to prove that it cannot be influenced are all being known.

> **Knowing does not need to defeat another position to prove that it is not trapped.**

Stop here. Do not establish another camp that is always right.

## Q10｜What Can This Theory Do in the Face of Death and Loss?

Loss can make past and future heavy at the same time.

The past says: “We used to be together.”

The future says: “Never again.”

In this moment, they close together into one sentence:

> **“I cannot bear it.”**

Stop for a moment. The past has passed; the future has not yet arrived.

What appears now is memory, absence, bodily pain, grief, and longing; an image of “never seeing him again” and the fear “What will I do without him?”

All of these are being known.

Look again at “I cannot bear it.” That too is being known.

Can the “I” being torn between past and future also be felt and described?

> **Who knows this entire experience of loss?**

Do not use “Luminosity neither arises nor ceases” to suppress grief. If a feeling appears—“I am seeing the grief, yet it is still about to swallow me”—that feeling, together with its fear, belongs to the known.

Grief does not have to stop first.

Ask one more question:

> **Grief is being known. Is the Luminosity by which all this is known swallowed by grief too? Loss occurs within the scene; has Knowing therefore become diminished as well?**

What can be pointed to now is longing, absence, pain in the body, and the self that can never go back to how things were. Do not leave the saddest character among them to answer on behalf of Knowing.

Tears can still flow.

> **The knowing of this grief does not mean Knowing is imprisoned within the grief too.**

Stop here. There is no need to give loss an explanation immediately.

## What This Chapter Is Actually Practicing

Ten questions have not been turned into ten rules for life.

They invite the same discernment in different situations: memory, expectation, emotion, identity, and the feeling “I am observing” can all be recognized as belonging to the known. Then what happens to these contents is no longer attributed to Knowing as well.

It is precisely this final step that is easiest to miss.

Consciousness says: “I know it belongs to the known, but the pain is still here, so I am still not free.”

Now even that sentence is being known.

> **Does pain not yet having ended mean that Knowing is not yet free?**

There is no need to answer with “I am already free.” The self eager to declare completion may be the next scene.

In this moment, the character within the scene is no longer made to occupy the final subject-position. Nor must the plot supply a satisfying ending before this identification is allowed to loosen.

Ease and release may appear. There is no need to grasp them as new proof of identity.

Freedom does not have to wait until every matter has been thought through correctly.

> **First loosen the absolute authority of the false self; wisdom then has an opportunity to emerge naturally.**

Stop. All of this, now, is being known.
